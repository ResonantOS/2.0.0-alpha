# Code of Conduct

## Our Standard

ResonantOS contributors, maintainers, testers, and agents must make project
spaces safe for focused technical collaboration. Expected behavior includes:

- treating people with respect regardless of identity, experience, or role;
- criticizing ideas and changes without attacking the person who proposed them;
- stating uncertainty, evidence, and conflicts of interest plainly;
- respecting privacy, consent, and human-only action boundaries; and
- accepting correction and taking responsibility for harmful conduct.

Unacceptable behavior includes harassment, threats, discrimination, sexualized
attention, deliberate intimidation, doxxing, sustained disruption, bad-faith
impersonation, or publishing another person's private information without
permission.

## Scope

This policy applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, project
boards, community discussions, and project-related private communication. It
also applies when someone publicly represents ResonantOS.

Automated agents act on behalf of the person operating them. The operator is
responsible for agent-generated comments and changes and must correct harmful
or disruptive output promptly.

## Reporting

Do not use a public issue to report harassment, private personal information,
or other sensitive conduct. Send a private email to the repository contact
listed in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) with the subject prefix `[CONDUCT]`.

Share the smallest useful account of the incident. Do not include credentials,
access tokens, private user content, or unrelated personal data. Reports will
be handled as confidentially as practical, but information may be shared with
the people needed to investigate and respond.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may remove content, issue a private correction, give a warning,
temporarily restrict participation, or permanently ban a participant. The
response should be proportionate to severity, impact, prior conduct, and
willingness to repair harm. Retaliation against a reporter or participant in an
investigation is itself a violation.

Maintainers with a conflict of interest must recuse themselves. Enforcement
decisions should be documented privately, including the facts considered and
the action taken.
